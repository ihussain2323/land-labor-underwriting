# Underwriting conventions from prior reviews

Use these observations for an early screen when the new deal supports them. Cite the current source or the applicable comparable in the private deal record. Keep the original meeting references and workbook versions with the deal team.

| Field | Observed treatment | What to verify on the new deal |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition and hold | Prior screens used a first-of-month acquisition and a four-year hold. One deal already under LOI had a three-to-five-month estimated close. | Use the LOI or purchase agreement and document the deal stage. Choose a closing date explicitly. Confirm loan maturity separately. |
| Leasing and renewals | Prior reviews used 70% renewal probability. | Tie current occupancy to the acquired units on the rent roll. Set projected leasing from the vacancy assumption through the exit and forward year. |
| Rent growth | A comparable used 2.75%. A subsequent review selected 3% market rent growth. | Record ZIP rent and value research separately. For regulated units, distinguish market rent from collectible rent. |
| Operating expenses | Start with seller actuals. For missing categories use a comparable residential expense per unit, less costs already booked for the same service. Grow included dollar expense lines by 3% per year unless documented otherwise. | Show the seller, adjusted and comparable cases on the same revenue and debt basis. Tie monthly T-12 detail to the statement. Report trailing NOI only with sufficient revenue and expense evidence. |
| Other income and renovation | Base cases require collections, billing support or a documented renovation plan and achievable rent premium. | Exclude unsupported receipts and premiums from the base. Show the basis and growth of included income. |
| Fees and reserves | A reviewed case used 3% of effective gross income for property management and 0.5% for asset management below NOI. Prior screens used replacement reserves of $250 per unit with 3% growth. | Verify the agreement, selector, expense base and placement relative to NOI. |
| Buyer and selling costs | Two NYC precedents used 3% buyer costs. Itemized budgets varied. Prior reviews used 4% selling costs. | Use a deal-specific budget when available. Reconcile tax, legal, diligence, reserves and debt fees without overlap. |
| Partner equity | Five of seven distinct precedents used 5% GP equity. The others used 10% and 20%. | Use the JV agreement when available. Apply the selected share to peak equity, then check GP and LP dollar contributions and distributions. |
| Debt | One acquisition screen used 65% of price, 5.50% fixed interest and a 1.25% upfront fee. Another used 48 months of interest-only payments. | Use lender terms when available. Confirm the purchase period receives the proceeds, the fee occurs once and the model repays the balance at the intended date. Month 0 funded acquisition debt in the reviewed master. An older in-place debt model used month 1. |
| Exit and taxes | A reviewed case used a provisional 5% exit cap. A tax-benefit deal required a dated transition to full taxes and a separate rent surcharge scenario. | Support exit pricing for the subject. Model a known tax increase even in the zero-surcharge case. Document eligibility, collection and timing before recognizing surcharge income. |

Start the investment review with acquisition price per unit and rentable square foot, entry NOI and cap rate, cash-on-cash yield, dated IRR and the LP check. Trace results through funding, holding period and exit. Check that beginning invested equity rolls from the prior ending balance for MFFO. Inspect dashboard links and the selected waterfall. In one master version, four formula errors in Transaction Inputs L56:L59 belonged to an inactive promote branch. Recheck dependencies in the actual deal copy.
